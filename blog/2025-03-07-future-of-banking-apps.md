# The Future of Fiance Apps: From Clunky Workflows to LLM-Powered Agents

For years, banking apps have forced users into rigid UI-driven workflows that prioritize software logic over user intent.

Consider something as simple as sending money. You open your banking app intending to send $250 to Miriam—a straightforward task. But before you can execute it, you need to:

1. Click on Pay & Transfer
2. Remember that Miriam uses Zelle and navigate there
3. Click Pay and find Miriam in a long list of contacts
4. Finally, enter the amount and confirm

And that’s assuming no interruptions. I just went through these steps in my mobile banking app and got two distracting pop-ups: one promoting a new account type, another informing me of a new Zelle recurring payments feature. Instead of streamlining the experience, today’s banking apps force users to learn and navigate UI complexity.

Now, imagine a different experience:

You simply say or type, *“Send $250 to Miriam.”*

Immediately, a pre-filled payment form appears, confirming the transaction via Zelle, ready for your authorization. No clicking through menus. No UI friction. No distractions.

This is the future of enterprise software: agentic LLM-driven interfaces that interpret user intent instead of requiring users to understand software workflows.

## LLMs and Agentic Interfaces: Translating User Intent into Action

The problem with traditional UI-based apps isn’t just that they require unnecessary steps—it’s that they expect users to adapt to software rather than software adapting to users.

LLMs (large language models) fundamentally change this dynamic. Instead of navigating an interface, users can state their intent directly.

In the banking example:

- Instead of hunting for the Zelle option, the LLM knows how to execute payments based on past transactions.
- Instead of scanning a contact list, it recognizes “Miriam” as a payment contact.
- Instead of distracting the user with irrelevant pop-ups, an intelligent agent waits for the right context to suggest relevant features.\
  For instance, after sending Miriam \$250 for the second month in a row, the LLM proactively asks: *"Would you like to set up a recurring payment?"*

That’s software adapting to users, not the other way around.

## Beyond Consumer Banking: Internal Finance Applications Are Even Worse

If consumer banking apps—designed with huge UX budgets and entire teams of engineers—are this inefficient, imagine the state of internal finance applications.

Banks, investment firms, and financial institutions rely on hundreds of internal tools for risk assessment, fraud detection, compliance, and customer service. These tools:

- Receive less budget and fewer UX improvements than customer-facing apps.
- Are built with rigid, UI-heavy interfaces that slow down workflows.
- Require employees to manually cross-reference multiple systems to complete tasks.

An agentic LLM interface could dramatically speed up internal operations—reducing manual work, improving accuracy, and increasing efficiency.

The shift from UI-driven workflows to agentic LLM-driven interfaces is inevitable. But to power this transition, enterprises need something beyond just a chatbot on top of legacy infrastructure—they need a neuro-symbolic data platform.

## The Neuro-Symbolic Data Platform: Why LLMs Alone Aren’t Enough

LLMs are excellent at understanding natural language intent, but they struggle with:

1. Interfacing with structured financial data (databases, APIs, transaction logs)
2. Executing precise, reliable operations (where mistakes are costly)
3. Maintaining security, auditability, and compliance

A purely neural AI approach (LLMs alone) is too unreliable for high-stakes financial transactions. A purely symbolic approach (traditional rules-based systems) is too rigid to handle human-like interactions.

The answer is a neuro-symbolic data platform—which combines:

- LLMs for natural-language reasoning and user interaction
- Symbolic (rules-based) systems for enforcing financial business logic, security, and compliance.

A neuro-symbolic data platform provides the following features that are required for AI applications within finance:

- Precise execution of financial operations
- Safe access to sensitive banking data
- Reliable enforcement of business rules and compliance
